Chapter 4: Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the set of metabolic reactions that convert chemical energy from nutrients into ATP (Smith et al., 2019, p. 34). It occurs in the cytoplasm and in the mitochondria, e.g. during glycolysis and the Krebs cycle.[1]

Glycolysis splits one glucose molecule into two pyruvate molecules [12]. Fig. 3 shows the ten enzymatic steps involved. Dr. Krebs described the citric acid cycle in 1937.

The electron transport chain, i.e. the final stage, produces about 34 ATP per glucose. See the reference diagram for details on oxidative phosphorylation.

Fermentation allows glycolysis to continue when oxygen is scarce. Lactic acid fermentation occurs in muscle cells during intense exercise.
